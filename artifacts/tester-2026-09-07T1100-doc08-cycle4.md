## The Medium — and it is mine, in the most instructive way yet

**This matrix pinned a 142-story backlog and published a 134-story census.** Doc 05 **v2.5.0**
states **142 stories**; §6's Stories row read `134 | 134 (all carry Gherkin AC) | 17 | 117`. So
"**134 traced of 134**" read as a *complete population* when the population is 142.

**I verified the finding independently before accepting it** — a mechanical id diff of every
`US-####` in Doc 05 against every `US-####` in this matrix. Eight ids appear **nowhere** in Doc 08
(`grep -c` returns 0 for each), and I resolved what each was minted for from Doc 05's v2.4.0 mint
line: **US-0135** (FR-121) · **US-0136** (FR-125) · **US-0137** (FR-133) · **US-0138** (FR-126) ·
**US-0139** (FR-127) · **US-0140** (FR-128) · **US-0141** (FR-129) · **US-0142** (FR-050).

**Where it came from, stated plainly because it is mine.** At v2.11.1 I advanced the BKLG pin
v2.3.0 → v2.5.0 as a **version-only** advance and annotated it "NOT re-read", adding that the
`US-####` chains were "**not** re-verified against v2.5.0 by it". That annotation was true, it was
careful, and **it was insufficient** — I then left a census derived from the *old* backlog standing
beside the *new* pin. The reviewer found precisely the thing my own disclaimer said was owed.

**Two lessons, and the second is the useful one:**
1. **An accurate disclaimer does not make an inaccurate number accurate.**
2. **A pin advance is a change to a denominator.** A version-only pin advance carries a duty to
   re-check every count that rests on the pinned document, *or* to say inside the count itself that
   it was not re-checked. I did neither. That is narrower and more actionable than "be careful",
   and it is what I wrote into the changelog.
