### 0.1 Read this first — this release is a practice run

Version 0.1.0 is a **public rehearsal**, not the finished product.

- The rules of the app are real. The **privacy protections are not switched on yet.**
- The checks that prove you are a real person are **being simulated** in this release, not enforced.
- **Please do not use this release to organise for real** if being known as a supporter could
  cost you your job, your safety or your family.

Two warnings matter most. Read them before you do anything else:

- **§2.6 — how private your vote is right now.** Your vote is anonymous, but in this version you
  are still able to prove to someone else how you voted. If anyone is pressuring you, this matters.
- **§0.2 — what Trumocracy is not.** It is not a government election.

### 0.2 What Trumocracy is — and what it is not

**Trumocracy helps people build and run political parties.**

You can:

- write a full party programme,
- gather public support for it,
- watch the party open by itself when enough people back it,
- join any open party,
- put ideas to the members, and
- vote on them, with exactly one vote each.

**Trumocracy is not a government election, and it never will be.**

- It does **not** run, count or certify any national, state or local government election.
- A result here is a **party's own decision** about its own affairs. It is **not** an official
  election result, and no government has to accept it.
- If your country requires a party to file named officers or audited accounts, Trumocracy can give
  you the record of what your party decided. **A person still files it, under their own name.**

Trumocracy also does **not**:

- sell memberships, votes or influence — nobody can pay for a bigger say;
- let anyone at Trumocracy approve, block, edit or delete a party, a member or a result;
- keep a list of who belongs to which party — that list does not exist anywhere;
- keep your identity documents, your fingerprints or your home address;
- moderate political opinions. (Where content breaks the law in one country, we can hide it from
  view **in that country only**, and every time we do that we write it in a public log.)

## 1. Get started — step by step

Three short tasks get you from nothing to taking part. Take them in order.

### 1.1 Before you begin

You need:

- a phone or a browser (see §3.3 for what is supported),
- a few minutes of quiet, and
- one way to prove you are a real person (§1.2 shows you the choices).

You do **not** need money, a bank account, a special app store, or any technical knowledge.

### 1.2 Task 1 — Prove you are a real person

You do this **once**. After that, you never do it again.

**Why it exists.** One person gets one vote. That promise only works if each person can take part
once. So the app has to be sure you are a real, unique adult — **without learning who you are.**

**What leaves your phone.** A **proof**, and nothing else.

> **What this means — a "proof"**
> A proof is a short piece of maths that says "this is a real, unique adult" and nothing more.
> Your passport, your photo, your date of birth and your document number stay on your phone or
> with the organisation that checked them. Trumocracy never receives them, never stores them, and
> so can never lose them, sell them or be forced to hand them over.

**Your choices.** Your area offers **at least two** independent ways to do this, and at least one
of them is **not run by a government**. You may use any one of them.

| Way to prove you are real | What it involves | Good to know |
|---|---|---|
| **Read your passport or national ID chip with your own phone** | Hold your phone against the chip in your document. Your phone reads it and makes the proof. | Strong. The document never leaves your hand. Needs a document with a chip. |
| **A verification meeting with a local organisation** | Go to a desk run by an accredited local civil-society organisation. A person checks you in the room. | The main path if you have no documents, or no signal at home. |
| **A one-off uniqueness check** | A check that confirms nobody else has already signed up as you. | Needs you to reach the place or device that does the check. |
| **A civil registry or electoral roll confirmation** | Your election commission or registry confirms you are on its list. | Only where the authorities take part. |
| **Neighbours who vouch for you** | Three people already verified in your area confirm that you are real. | The last-resort way in. It is the weakest, so it is used less often and is limited in how many people may join this way. |

**Steps**

1. Open the app and read the first screen. It tells you plainly what is kept and what is not.
   You must tick to say you have read it. → *You should see a short list, in your own language.*
2. Choose one of the ways above. → *You should see only the ways that are live in your area.*
3. Follow that path. This may be on your phone, or in person.
4. Set up your sign-in. You use the **fingerprint or face unlock you already use on your phone**.
   → *You should see "You are signed up."*

✅ **You are now verified.** There is nothing to write down, nothing to remember and nothing to lose.

**Please also do this now:** add a **second device** (a second phone, a tablet or a computer) and
choose your **helpers** for getting back in. See §2.9. Doing it now takes two minutes. Doing it after
you lose your phone takes a week.

#### If you have no accepted document

Be honest with yourself about this step: **some people will find it hard.** A document check is easy
if you have a modern passport and difficult if you have never had one — and that often falls on the
people this platform exists to serve. We are not going to pretend otherwise.

If you have no accepted document:

1. **Use the in-person path.** Local organisations run verification desks exactly for this. They are
   listed in the app under "Ways to prove you are real".
2. **Use neighbours who vouch for you**, if your area offers it.
3. **If you are refused, you can appeal.** An appeal never asks you for more personal information
   than the first check did.

You may also be told that your area is **not open yet**. An area only opens when it has at least two
independent ways to verify people, so that no single organisation can decide who is allowed to take
part.

**One thing to know.** The organisation that checks you does learn that you signed up for something.
It does not learn what you do afterwards, which party you join, or how you vote. To reduce even that,
**sign up early — well before you plan to do anything.**

### 1.3 Task 2 — Show where you live, without telling anyone your address

Some things only make sense locally. Only people who live in an area should decide that area's
affairs. So the app needs to know your **area** — and only your area.

**What a residency confirmation is.** It is a note from a trusted local organisation that says
"this person lives in this area". It names the **area**, never the street.

> **What this means — "area"**
> An area is a place on a published list, like *country ▸ region ▸ city ▸ district ▸ ward*.
> The smallest area the app can handle is a **ward**, because that is the smallest area an
> election needs. The app has **no way to store a street, a postcode, a map pin or a location**.
> Not even a scrambled one.

**Who can give you one**

- your election commission or civil registry,
- a bank or phone company that already knows your address,
- an accredited local organisation running a verification desk,
- or three verified neighbours who vouch that you live there (the last-resort path).

**Steps**

1. In the app, choose **Confirm where I live**.
2. Pick one of the organisations offered for your area.
3. Follow their check. → *You should see "Your area is confirmed: <your area>."*

✅ **Done.** Trumocracy now knows you are a resident of that area, and nothing more.

**You can change your area, but not often.** You may hold **one** area at a time. After a change,
you must wait **180 days** before changing again. This stops people moving from place to place to
swing local votes.

#### Why the app sometimes covers a wider area than your own ward

Sometimes the app tells you that your action will be published under a **larger area** than your
ward — your city, for example, instead of your street's ward.

This is a protection, not a mistake.

If only 40 verified people live in your ward, and the record shows "someone in this ward acted",
anyone can guess it was you. Being one of 40 is not anonymous. So the app uses this rule:

> An action is only published once **at least 1,000 verified people** in that area could have been
> the one who did it. If your ward is smaller than that, the app uses the next area up.

If your area is too small even then, the app **holds your action back** and tells you plainly that
publishing is delayed and why. It does not quietly publish something that points at you.

This changes **where your action is shown**. It does not change **who is allowed to act** — your
rights still follow the area where you actually live.

### 1.4 Task 3 — Back a party you agree with

This is the fastest way to see the whole thing work.

1. Open **Petitions** and read one. → *You should see the eight chapters of its programme, how many
   people have backed it, and how many are needed.*
2. Tap **Back this petition**.
3. Confirm. → *You should see the count go up by one.*

✅ **You have taken part.** Before you do this, read §2.2 — your support is **public by default**.

### 1.5 What next

- Start your own party → §2.1
- Join an open party → §2.3
- Have your say → §2.4, and the warning in §2.6
- Get back in after losing your phone → §2.9

### 2.1 Start a new party

Nobody approves your party. Nobody can refuse it. The only thing standing between your idea and a
working party is **whether enough people who live in your area back it.**

#### Step 1 — Write the programme: all eight chapters

Your programme must cover **all eight** of these:

1. **Finance** — money, tax, spending
2. **Society** — how people live together
3. **Governance** — how decisions get made
4. **Law** — justice, courts, policing rules
5. **Education** — schools and learning
6. **Healthcare** — health and care
7. **Security** — safety and defence
8. **Regional Plans** — what you will do in your own area

**All eight, or you cannot publish.** A party is a whole programme, not a slogan.

Each chapter has to contain a published minimum amount of writing. The app shows you that standard
**before** you start writing, and a progress bar as you go. If you try to publish with a chapter
missing or too thin, the app **names the chapters that are short**. It never judges your politics —
the standard is about how much you wrote, never about what you believe.

**You can write offline.** On a weak connection, keep writing. Your work is kept on your phone and
sent when you get signal back.

**Your name is not attached.** While you draft and petition, you appear under a made-up name of your
choosing. You choose later, and separately, whether to become a public figure (see §2.7).

#### Step 2 — Choose your area and your name

- Pick **one** area. This is the area your party serves, and the area whose people decide it.
- Pick a name and an emblem. If another petition or party in that area already uses them, the app
  refuses and tells you which one has it.
- **The area is permanent.** After your party opens, it cannot be changed. If you later want to work
  in another area, you run a fresh petition there and meet that area's own bar.

#### Step 3 — Open the petition

Your party goes live as a **petition**: a public page where people who live in your area can back it.

- The petition runs for a window you choose, between **30 days and 365 days**.
- Everything is public: the programme, the count, the bar, and the time left.
- Nobody is ever shown who backed it, and no list of people who did *not* back it exists.

#### Step 4 — "Enough support" — how the number is worked out

The number of backers you need is worked out by the app, in public, and **nobody can change it for
your party** — not us, not a rival, not an official.

It is the **largest** of these three numbers:

| Part of the sum | What it means |
|---|---|
| A set share of your area's population | The main bar. The share is **2% by default**. |
| The same share of the **verified people** in your area | Stops anyone making the bar easy by understating the population. |
| **500 backers** | An absolute floor. No party is ever founded on a handful of accounts. |

More detail, plainly:

- The share can be set anywhere between **0.5% and 20%**, depending on the area.
- **The exact share for each area is set per area and published before any petition opens there.**
  It is not decided later and never changed under a live petition.
- The population figure comes from **at least five independent published sources**, and the app uses
  the middle value, so one wrong or manipulated source cannot move it. That figure has a **7-day
  window** during which anyone can challenge it before it is used.
- **The bar is frozen the moment your petition opens.** Nobody can raise it under a petition that
  is succeeding, or lower it for a friend.

#### Step 5 — The party opens by itself

When the count reaches the number needed, the party **opens**. That is all there is to it.

- **Nobody approves it.** There is no review, no committee and no waiting room. The code has no
  button for approving, rejecting, featuring or delaying a party, because we did not build one.
- Anyone can trigger the final step, including you, including a stranger.
- The moment it opens, the app records permanently: the programme as written, the number of
  backers, the population figure used, and where that figure came from.

If your window runs out before you reach the number, the petition is **archived**. It stays readable
forever. You may try again, but not immediately with the same text in the same area — there is a
waiting period, and the app tells you the earliest date.

### 2.2 Support a party's petition

**Steps**

1. Open the petition and read the eight chapters.
2. Tap **Back this petition**.
3. Confirm. → *The count goes up by one.*

**Rules**

- You may back a petition **only in the area where you live**.
- **One person, one backing.** A second attempt from any device changes nothing.
- Backing cannot be sold, lent, given away or done on someone else's behalf. There is no feature
  for it, so there is nothing for a broker to buy.

#### ⚠️ Your support is public by default in this version

Backing a founding petition is a **public act**, on purpose. It is closer to signing a public
petition in the street than to casting a secret ballot. Public backing is what gives a petition its
weight.

What that means for you:

- **You back it pseudonymously.** You prove you live in the area. Your real name is not shown.
- **But the act itself is on the public record**, and in a small area, or if people already know
  your made-up name, that can be enough for someone to work out who you are.
- A **fully private** way to back a petition exists for high-risk places. It is **not switched on in
  this version.**

**So: only back a petition if you are comfortable being seen to support it.**

Voting inside a party is different, and is covered in §2.4 and §2.6.

#### You can withdraw before the party opens

- You may withdraw at any time **before** the party opens. The count drops by one, and nobody is
  told who withdrew.
- After the party opens, you cannot withdraw. Opening is final. The app will tell you so.

Withdrawing removes your support. It does not stop you joining the party later if you change your
mind again.

### 2.3 Join a party

Joining is the simplest thing in Trumocracy.

**Steps**

1. Open the party's page.
2. Tap **Join**.

That is it. **You are a member straight away.**

- **No approval.** No one reviews you.
- **No interview.** No one questions you.
- **No invitation, no sponsor, no fee, no membership tier.**
- **Nobody can throw you out.** No founder, no office-holder, no funder and nobody at Trumocracy has
  the power to remove, suspend or block a member. That power does not exist in the app.

**Every member has exactly one vote. Always.**

Not one and a half for founders. Not two for donors. Not extra for long service or for holding a
position. One. The app has no way to make one member's vote count more than another's, and any
attempt to set one up is refused.

**Who can see that you are a member?** Nobody. The party's **total** membership and its breakdown by
area are public. **Individual membership is never shown, exported or logged** — there is no member
list to leak, to sell, or to hand to anyone who demands one.

**One thing that does depend on time:** how long you have been a member decides **which kinds of
decision you may vote on** — not how much your vote weighs. See §2.4.

### 2.4 Have your say: proposals and voting

Any member can put an idea to the party. Nobody screens it, delays it, hides it or reorders it.

#### The four kinds of decision

The bigger the decision, the more agreement it needs and the longer it takes. There are four kinds.

| Kind of decision | Examples | How many members must take part | How many must say yes | How long you must have been a member | Talk about it first | Voting lasts at least | Wait before it happens |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Everyday** | Publish a statement, arrange an event | 5% | More than half | No wait — from day one | — | 3 days | None |
| **Policy** | Adopt a position, agree a budget within the cap | 10% | More than half | 14 days | 2 days | 3 days | 48 hours |
| **Structural** | Change a chapter of the programme, change how positions work | 25% | 60% | 90 days | 7 days | 7 days | 14 days |
| **Founding rules** | The founding document, the party's name, its core values, the rules for changing the rules, closing the party | 40% | 75% | 180 days | 14 days | 14 days | 30 days |

Read the table like this:

- **"How many members must take part"** is the minimum turnout. If fewer than that vote, the
  decision fails — even if everyone who voted said yes.
- **"How many must say yes"** is the share of those who voted.
- Both bars, plus the closing time, are shown to you **before you vote**, and **cannot be changed
  once voting has opened**. If someone tries, the attempt is refused and written down in public.

A party may make these rules **stricter** for itself. It may **never** make them weaker than the
table above.

#### Why the founding rules are harder to change

The founding rules are what the party *is*. If a simple majority on a quiet Tuesday could rewrite
them, then any group that turned up in numbers could take the party away from the people who built
it. That has happened to real parties for a century.

So three protections apply to founding rules, and only to founding rules:

1. **A high bar.** 40% must take part and 75% must agree.
2. **A waiting time before you may vote on them: 180 days of membership.** This is why you may vote
   on everyday party business from your first day, but not on the founding rules until you have been
   part of the community for a while. No party may set this below **90 days**.
3. **A long delay before the change happens: 30 days.** Everyone can see it coming and can argue, or
   leave.

**Important: waiting time changes what you may vote on. It never changes how much your vote
weighs.** A member of 200 days and a member of 181 days have exactly the same power. This is not
seniority. It is closer to a residency requirement, applied to the founding document alone.

**A party can also lock some clauses at founding.** If the founders write "we will never take
corporate donations" as a locked clause, no later majority can undo it. The app enforces it.

**If a party grows very fast, big votes get harder.** If membership grows by more than **20% in 30
days**, then for structural and founding-rule votes the app automatically:

- **doubles** the voting window,
- raises the share who must agree by **5 percentage points**, and
- publishes a warning that every member sees.

This is not aimed at newcomers. Genuine growth is a good thing. It is aimed at a **sudden** flood
that arrives just in time to win a vote. The extra protection fades away after 90 days.

#### The discussion period

Before voting opens, there is a **discussion period** — 2 days for policy, 7 days for structural,
14 days for founding rules. The full text is published and cannot be edited once voting opens.

The person who made the proposal may fix or withdraw it **during** the discussion period. Once
voting opens, the text is locked.

> Trumocracy has no forum, no messaging and no comment threads. Parties talk on their own channels.
> The discussion period is time, not a chat room.

#### How to vote

1. Open the proposal. → *You should see the kind of decision, both bars, and when voting closes.*
2. Choose your answer.
3. Confirm. → *You should see "Your vote was accepted."*

**Who can vote on this proposal is fixed at the moment it opens.** If you join, or reach the
required membership time, after that moment, you cannot vote on that particular proposal. This is
what stops a group joining after they have read a proposal in order to defeat it.

**You will see no running total.** No partial counts, no turnout by option, no predictions —
nothing until voting closes. Nobody can pressure you with "we are winning" or "you may as well not
bother".

#### The waiting period before a decision takes effect

A decision that passes does not happen at once. It waits: **nothing** for everyday decisions, **48
hours** for policy, **14 days** for structural, **30 days** for founding rules.

During the wait, the change is publicly listed as pending, with the date it will take effect.
**Nobody can shorten, skip or cancel that wait.** It is your time to argue, to organise, or to leave.

#### After voting closes

The result is published in plain language: the outcome, how many took part, whether the turnout bar
was met, and whether the agreement bar was met. Anyone can re-count it from the public record — you
do not have to take our word for it (see §2.12).

### 2.5 Publish what your party promises

A party can publish its promises with dates, and mark each one **in progress**, **met** or **not
met**.

- Every version is kept **forever**. Editing creates a new version; the old one stays readable, and
  you can compare any two versions side by side.
- **No one can delete or rewrite a published version** — not the party, not an office-holder, not
  Trumocracy. Quiet rewriting of promises is impossible here.
- Trumocracy never rates, scores or fact-checks a promise. Only the party sets a status, and every
  change of status is public.

### 2.6 ⚠️ An honest warning about voting privacy in this version

**Please read this section fully. It is the most important paragraph in this guide.**

Your vote in this version is **anonymous**. It is **not yet receipt-free**. These are two different
promises, and the difference could matter to your safety.

| | What it means | True in version 0.1.0? |
|---|---|---|
| **Anonymous** | Someone reading the public record cannot tell that **you** voted, or which way. | **Yes.** |
| **Receipt-free** | **Even if you wanted to**, you could not prove to another person how you voted. | **No. Not yet.** |

**In plain words:** nobody can find out how you voted by themselves. But if someone stands over you
and demands that you show them how you voted, **in this version you are able to show them.** And
because you *can*, they can insist that you do.

That is exactly the situation this platform exists to protect you from — a boss, a landlord, a
spouse, a clan leader, a party official or a vote-buyer who wants proof. **In this version, the
protection against the onlooker is in place. The protection against the person pressuring you is
not.**

We are not going to soften this. It is a real gap, it is why this release is a rehearsal, and it is
the single biggest reason not to use version 0.1.0 for real political organising where being
identified could hurt you.

#### What protection is coming, and when

The missing protection works like this: **you will be able to change your vote later, secretly.**

- You will be able to vote again, quietly, at any time before voting closes.
- **Only your last vote will count.**
- **Nothing** will show that you changed it — not the public record, not your phone, not any
  message. A later vote will look exactly like a first vote to anyone watching.

That is what makes a threat useless. Someone can stand over you and watch you vote their way, and
then have **no way to check** whether you changed it after they left. A promise that cannot be
checked cannot be enforced, so there is no point paying for it or threatening for it.

To make this work, votes are kept sealed until the count, and the count is done jointly by a
**committee of at least seven independent organisations in different countries, five of whom must
act together** to produce the result. No single one of them — and no small group — can open a single
ballot. Ever.

**When:** this arrives in **Phase 3**, and it is currently switched off. When it is switched on, the
current way of voting **closes at the same moment**. Both cannot be open at once, because a person
pressuring you would simply demand that you use the provable one.

You can check the current state yourself: the app shows the warning on **every voting screen** for
as long as this protection is off.

#### What to do meanwhile if someone is pressuring you

1. **Do not use version 0.1.0 to vote on anything that could put you at risk.** This release is a
   rehearsal. Treat it as one.
2. **If you are pressured, your safety comes first.** Nothing in this app is worth being hurt for.
3. **Be careful what you back publicly.** Backing a petition is public by design (§2.2). Party
   membership and votes are not shown — but the ability to prove your own vote is the gap described
   above.
4. **Protect your phone.** Someone holding your unlocked phone can act as you. Add a second device
   (§2.9), keep your unlock private, and tell your helpers if your phone is taken.
5. **Wait for the protection to arrive** before organising in a place where being known would be
   dangerous.

#### Two more honest limits in this version

- **Running totals can be read by a determined person.** The app and its search service hide interim
  results from everyone, but the underlying public record cannot hide what it stores. A technically
  skilled observer can read a running count before voting closes. This is disclosed, not fixed.
- **The identity checks are simulated in this release.** In 0.1.0 the checks that prove personhood
  and residency are stand-ins that accept anything. So the "one person, one vote" and privacy
  guarantees are **demonstrated, not enforced**, in this release. Real checks arrive in Phase 2.

### 2.7 Stand for a position

> **Not available in version 0.1.0.** Standing for a position is switched off in this release and
> arrives in **Phase 3**. This section describes how it will work, so you can decide in advance.

**Only you can put your name forward.** There are no lists drawn up by anyone, no nominations of
other people, and no appointments. If a member tries to nominate someone else, the app refuses.

**You stand only where you live.** You may stand for a position covering your own area. If you try
to stand somewhere you do not live, the app refuses. If you move, your candidacy for the old area
ends — although a term you are already serving runs to its end.

**You need a minimum of local backing.** A published minimum number of members who live in your area
must back your nomination before you appear on a ballot. Backing from people outside the area does
not count. This keeps ballots serious without letting anyone gatekeep them.

**You give up your anonymity on purpose, and it lasts.** This is the one place where Trumocracy
shows a real name — and only because **you** chose it.

Before your candidacy is published, you go through a **separate consent step**. It states, before
you agree:

- your **real-world identity becomes public**;
- this is **irreversible** for your candidacy **and for any term you then serve**;
- the only way to take it back is to **withdraw before the ballot locks**;
- once the ballot locks, you cannot withdraw and the disclosure stands.

**If you do not give that consent, nothing about you is published.** Ever.

Ordinary members are never named. Not in any screen, any export, any log or any public record. The
difference is deliberate: **anonymity protects the powerless; openness binds the powerful.** Asking
people to vote for you is asking for power, and the price is being known.

**Voting for a position** is done only by members who live in that area. The timetable, the list of
candidates and the tie-break rule are all published **before** voting opens and cannot be changed
afterwards. When voting closes, **the winner takes the position automatically.** Nobody confirms it,
ratifies it or can refuse to seat them.

Positions have a fixed length set by the party. At the end, the position ends automatically and a
fresh vote is needed.

**A representative's votes are public.** When someone votes **as an office-holder**, that vote is
published with their name. When the same person votes **as an ordinary member**, that vote stays
anonymous like everyone else's.

### 2.8 Vote out someone who is not doing the job

> **Not available in version 0.1.0.** Recall is switched off in this release and arrives in
> **Phase 3**. This section describes how it will work.

You do not have to wait for the next scheduled vote to remove a representative who has stopped
delivering.

**Who can start it.** Any member who lives in that position's area and has been a member long
enough. You need **nobody's permission** — not the representative's, not another office-holder's,
not the founder's, and not Trumocracy's. Nobody can block, hide or delay it. The app has no such
power.

**It takes two stages.**

1. **Signatures.** Enough members in the area must sign to open a recall vote.
2. **A vote, with a higher bar than the one that put them in.** If someone was elected with 55%
   support and the recall bar is 60%, then a recall vote at 58% **fails** and they stay. A later
   recall vote at 62% **succeeds**.

Both bars are published **before** signatures open and cannot be changed once the process starts.

**If a recall succeeds**, the position is removed automatically — no confirmation step, and no way
for the person to cling on — and a fresh vote for that position opens within a published number of
days.

**Protection from harassment.** There is a grace period after someone is elected during which no
recall may be started, and a cooling-off period after a failed recall before the same position can
be targeted again. Recall is meant to be accountability, not a way to grind someone down.

### 2.9 Get back in after losing your phone

You were never given a secret code to write down, so there is nothing you can have lost. Getting
back in works through **people you trust**.

#### Do this now, before anything goes wrong

1. **Add a second device.** A second phone, a tablet or a computer. Most "I broke my phone"
   situations are then solved in seconds and never touch the process below.
2. **Choose your helpers.** These are people you trust, each of whom already uses Trumocracy. You
   can also use a security key or a printed card kept somewhere safe.

#### If you have lost your phone

1. Open Trumocracy on any device and choose **I have lost my access**.
2. Ask enough of your helpers to confirm it is really you.
3. **Wait 7 days.**
4. After the wait, your account is handed back to you, on your new device.

#### Why it takes a week

Because a thief would also like it to be fast.

During those seven days:

- a notice goes to you, so you know it is happening; and
- **you can cancel it** with any device or key you still have.

So if someone steals your phone and tries to take your account, you stop them by tapping cancel. If
recovery were quick and pleasant, it would also be quick and easy to abuse. We chose slow, and we
would rather tell you why than pretend it is a technical necessity.

**Your helpers learn nothing.** Not which parties you belong to, not how you voted, not what you
have backed, not anything about your political life. All they do is confirm you are you. The same
is true of anyone else involved. Getting back in is not a side door into your privacy.

### 2.10 Leave a party

1. Open the party's page.
2. Tap **Leave**.

**You have left.** Right away.

- **Nobody can stop you.** No approval, no notice period, no penalty, no exit interview. The app
  has no way to delay or block it.
- Your rights in that party end at the same moment.
- You may join another party. You may join the same one again later.

Leaving is not just a convenience. It is the strongest check any member has: a party that stops
listening loses the people it is made of.

### 2.11 Start a new party from an old one (forking)

> **Not available in version 0.1.0.** Forking is switched off in this release and arrives in
> **Phase 3**. This section describes how it will work.

If a group inside a party disagrees deeply with where it is going, they do not have to win the
argument or give up. They can take the founding document and the history and start again.

**How it will work**

- **One in ten members** (10%) is enough to start a fork. You do **not** need permission from the
  party's leadership or its majority, and there is no function anywhere that lets them block it.
- The new party **inherits** the founding document and the record of promises, at a point in time
  you choose. Members can choose to come with it.
- There is a **30-day cooling-off period** before the fork goes ahead. A month is long enough for a
  hot argument to cool and short enough that it is a real option.
- The new party then **runs its own petition and must meet the full bar on its own** — exactly like
  any brand-new party. A fork gets a head start on writing, never on support.
- The link is permanent and public. Both parties show the fork in their history forever, so anyone
  can see which party continues what.

**Why this exists.** Any defence against a takeover eventually loses to someone determined enough.
Forking changes what a takeover is worth: capturing a party no longer captures its people. They
leave, with the programme and the history, and the captured shell keeps the empty structure.

The cost is real, and we will say it: easy exit means parties can splinter at the first
disagreement. The 10% bar, the waiting month and the requirement to earn support from scratch are
there to keep that cost in check.

### 2.12 If something goes wrong

#### You lost your phone

See §2.9. It works through people you trust, and it takes seven days.

#### Your proof failed, or you were refused

1. **Try another way.** Every area offers at least two independent ways to prove you are real. If a
   document check fails, an in-person desk may work.
2. **Check your document is still valid.** Residency confirmations expire; the app will ask you to
   renew.
3. **Appeal.** An appeal never asks for more personal information than the first check did.
4. **Your area may not be open yet.** An area only opens when it has at least two independent
   verifiers, one of them not run by a government. Until then the app says so plainly.

#### The app will not load

You should always be able to reach Trumocracy, even if the usual way is blocked.

1. **Try the other published addresses.** The app is published from several independent places at
   once, and every copy is identical. The current list of addresses ships with each release.
2. **Enter a different service address in Settings.** The app can use another service to send your
   actions. You may point it wherever you like; it checks the answers it gets rather than trusting
   them.
3. **If your action does not go through**, the app will try other routes by itself, including a
   slower route that no single operator can block.
4. **Nothing is lost while you are offline.** Drafts are kept on your phone and sent when you have
   signal.

#### Your action is queued, or delayed

- **"This will be published later"** — there are not yet enough verified people in your area for
  your action to be published without pointing at you. See §1.3. The app tells you why and publishes
  it when it is safe to.
- **"Your action is in a queue"** — you have used up your share of the free allowance for this
  period. **Your action is not refused and you are never charged.** It waits, and the app tells you
  roughly how long.

#### A decision looks wrong to you

You can check it yourself. You do not need an account, and you do not need our help.

1. **Open the result.** It shows the outcome, how many took part, and whether each bar was met.
2. **Open the record.** Every action ever taken — every backing, every join, every vote, every
   result, every version of every document — is published and cannot be altered afterwards. If
   anyone alters a record, anyone else can detect it.
3. **Re-count it.** All the figures behind a result are public, so a journalist, a researcher or a
   neighbour can add them up again and compare. A ready-made checking tool for non-experts is **not
   part of this release**; it is planned.
4. **Read the version history.** Founding documents and promises keep every version. If a party
   changed what it said, you can see exactly what changed and when.

**What we cannot do:** reverse a party's decision. There is no undo button, for anyone, including
us. If a party makes a decision that is unwise, the ways to answer it are the party's own rules
(propose a change), your right to leave (§2.10), and — later — forking (§2.11). We know this is
uncomfortable. It is the price of nobody being able to reverse a decision they dislike.

## 4. Why it works this way

### 4.1 The idea in one page

Trumocracy separates two things that political gatekeeping normally welds together: **proving you
are a real, eligible person**, and **revealing who you are**.

You prove once that you are a unique adult living in a particular area. From then on, the platform
only ever knows that "one eligible person in this area acted". It never knows which person, and it
cannot tell that the same person also acted somewhere else.

Everything after that is arithmetic that runs by itself:

- a count that either crosses a published bar or does not,
- a membership that anyone can join and nobody can veto,
- a vote that is one per person and cannot be sold, lent or given away,
- and a removal that happens automatically when the bar is met.

There is no override button, because we did not build one.

### 4.2 Why there is nobody to appeal to

Every time this product hit a hard problem, the easy fix was an admin button. Every time, we said no.

If a person at Trumocracy could approve, reject, promote, demote, delete or reorder anything inside
a party, we would have rebuilt the thing we set out to abolish — and that button would be demanded
by every government, every court and every angry founder, and eventually used.

The honest cost is this: **a party can make a decision that is unwise, or the result of a mistake,
and nobody outside that party can undo it.** Your remedies are the party's own rules, your right to
leave, and — later — the right to fork. We would rather tell you that than pretend otherwise.

### 4.3 Your privacy: what is public, what is private, and what we could never hand over

| Thing | Who can see it |
|---|---|
| A party's programme, founding rules and promises | **Everyone**, forever, including every past version |
| The number of people backing a petition | **Everyone** |
| **That you personally backed a petition** | **On the public record** — under a made-up name, but visible. See §2.2. |
| A party's total membership, and its breakdown by area | **Everyone** |
| **That you personally are a member** | **Nobody.** There is no member list anywhere. |
| Proposals, results, turnout and every count | **Everyone** |
| **How you voted** | **Nobody** — but see §2.6 for what is not yet protected |
| A candidate's or office-holder's real name | **Everyone** — because they chose it, in a separate consent step |
| An office-holder's votes made in that role | **Everyone** |
| **An ordinary member's votes** | **Nobody**, including that person's own party |
| Your identity documents, fingerprints or face scan | **Nobody** — we never receive them and never store them |
| Your home address, postcode or location | **Nobody** — the app has no place to put one |
| Your date of birth, name, phone number or email | **Nobody** — none of these exist on the public record |

**What happens if a government orders us to hand things over.** We answer honestly, and the honest
answer is mostly "we do not have it".

| What we are ordered to produce | What we can actually produce |
|---|---|
| "The member list for party X" | **Nothing useful.** No names, no addresses, no device details. The list does not exist. |
| "Tell us who cast this vote" | **Nothing.** Ballots are sealed under a key split across at least five different countries. We hold no part of it that could open one. |
| "Take down party Y" | **We cannot.** There is no pause button, no admin key and no override. We can be forced to block the website we run; the platform itself carries on. |
| "Stop this citizen taking part" | **We cannot.** There is no power over an individual account anywhere in the system. |

This is not defiance for its own sake. It is deliberately not building powers that, if they existed,
would be demanded and used — including against us. **The safest design for you is one where we are
genuinely powerless.**

**Where we are still exposed, stated plainly:**

- The organisation that verified you **knows you signed up for something**. It does not know what
  you did afterwards. In a small place, that fact alone may be a hint. Sign up early, before you
  plan to act.
- Somebody who can watch all internet traffic could try to match up timings. The 1,000-person rule
  reduces this. It does not remove it.
- A government can block the platform in its country. There are other ways in (§2.12), but this is a
  real cost to you, not a solved problem.
- **A published record cannot be deleted.** Your contribution to a party's programme is permanent.
  That is why authorship is under a made-up name by default: the permanence attaches to the name you
  chose, not to you.

### 4.4 Why money cannot buy a party here

There is nothing to buy.

- Membership cannot be purchased.
- Votes are not things. They cannot be transferred, sold, lent, given away or inherited, and no
  such feature exists to be exploited.
- No donation of any size gives anyone standing, priority, a better place on a ballot or an extra
  vote.

**We will be honest about what we do not stop.** A rich person can still buy advertising, hire
canvassers and try to persuade people. That is a real advantage and we cannot remove it. What we
remove is the shortcut: turning money directly into control of the party machine. And once
receipt-free voting arrives (§2.6), the oldest shortcut of all — paying people for their votes —
stops working, because the payer has no way to check.

### 4.5 What Trumocracy will not build

- **Vote delegation.** Handing your vote to someone else brings brokers back, and brokers are the
  problem we are solving.
- **Feeds, forums and messaging.** Parties use their own channels.
- **Moderation of political opinion by our staff.** A platform that judges politics is a political
  actor, and whoever writes those rules becomes the new gatekeeper. Where content is unlawful in one
  country, we can hide it **there**, we log it publicly, and the record itself is never deleted.
- **Running government elections.** Never. See §0.2.

## 5. Using Trumocracy with a screen reader, a keyboard, or in your own language

- **Every main task works with a screen reader** and with a keyboard or switch, and works at 200%
  text size.
- **Every main task meets WCAG 2.2 Level AA.** (In this practice release the accessibility audit has
  not yet been completed — the target is zero failures before launch.)
- **Eight languages at launch**, including a right-to-left language. No main task will show you an
  untranslated word. Dates, numbers and names use your local format.
- **Built for a slow connection and an old phone.** Every main task works on a five-year-old phone
  and a very weak connection. You can write offline; your work is sent when signal returns.
- **Nothing you must remember.** No codes, no phrases, no passwords to memorise.
