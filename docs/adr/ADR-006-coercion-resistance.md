# ADR-006 — Coercion resistance: MACI-style receipt-freeness with a threshold coordinator committee

```
Status:        Accepted
Date:          2026-08-08
Owner:         Ravi Deshmukh (Principal Architect)
Traces:        BR-005, FR-030..FR-035, NFR-003, RISK-02, RISK-10
```

## Context

This is the hardest problem in the system, and the one most decentralised-voting projects quietly
fail.

On a public ledger, a naive private vote is **not** coercion-resistant even when it is anonymous.
If I can show you my transaction, my secret, my proof, or my device, I can *prove* how I voted — and
the moment I can prove it, someone can pay me for it or threaten me for it. Anonymity protects
against the *observer*; it does nothing against the *briber*, because in vote-buying **the victim is
a willing participant in the proof**.

The property we actually need is **receipt-freeness**: even a voter who *wants* to prove how they
voted must be unable to. And stronger, **coercion resistance**: a voter under physical duress must be
able to comply visibly and defect invisibly.

Note also the perverse interaction with the product's own transparency goal: "all votes are
verifiable" must mean *the tally is verifiable*, never *individual ballots are attributable*. Doc 01
FAQ states this explicitly because it is the most common misreading of the vision.

## Decision

Adopt the **MACI** (Minimal Anti-Collusion Infrastructure) construction for all *member votes* —
proposals, charter amendments, candidate selection, recalls — with a **threshold coordinator
committee** replacing MACI's single coordinator.

### How it defeats bribery

1. Each voter registers a **voting public key** in the party's state tree.
2. Votes are submitted as **encrypted messages** to the coordinator's public key. Nobody can read
   them.
3. A voter may submit a **key-change message** at any time before the deadline, and **later messages
   from the new key override earlier ones**. Crucially, a key-change message is *indistinguishable
   from an ordinary vote message* to any observer.
4. Therefore: a briber who watches you cast "vote for X" cannot know whether you subsequently
   changed your key and re-voted. **The receipt is worthless.** Since the briber cannot verify
   compliance, the rational briber does not pay — the market for votes collapses rather than being
   policed.
5. A coerced voter under duress does the same: comply in front of the coercer, then silently
   override afterwards. This is why the voting window MUST be long (≥72h, NFR-003) and why the
   UI MUST NOT display any "you voted for X" confirmation that could be shown to a coercer — the
   client shows only "your ballot was accepted" with a **plausible-deniability screen** that looks
   identical for every choice (SCR-VOTE-CONFIRM, and a **panic re-vote** gesture, DES-063).
6. At close, the coordinator committee proves in zero knowledge that the published tally is the
   correct decryption and aggregation of the message queue. Anyone can verify the tally proof.
   Nobody learns any individual ballot.

### Replacing the single coordinator

Vanilla MACI's coordinator *can decrypt individual votes* (it cannot forge or censor them — those
are constrained by the proofs). For a platform whose users may be dissidents, a single party that
can read everyone's ballot is unacceptable: it is one subpoena, one rubber hose, or one insider away
from a complete political-affiliation database.

**Decision: an n-of-m threshold coordinator committee.** The coordinator key is generated by
**distributed key generation (DKG)** across a committee of ≥7 independent operators (civil-society
orgs, universities, and party-nominated observers from *different* parties), with a **5-of-7
threshold** required to compute the tally. No single member — and no minority coalition — can decrypt
anything.

Committee composition rules that make collusion structurally hard:
- Members are drawn from **different legal jurisdictions** (so no single court order reaches a
  threshold) and **competing parties** (so no single political interest reaches a threshold).
- Membership is **per-election, sampled fresh**, so there is no standing committee to capture.
- Committee members publish **liveness attestations**; failure to participate is slashable and
  triggers automatic replacement before the tally.

### Where MACI is *not* used

- **Petition endorsements** are **public by design.** Endorsing a new party's founding petition is a
  political act closer to signing a public petition than to casting a secret ballot, and public
  endorsement is what gives a petition social legitimacy. The citizen is warned clearly (Doc 14) and
  MAY endorse **pseudonymously** (proof-of-residency without identity), which is the default. A
  *fully private* endorsement mode is available as a charter option for high-risk jurisdictions
  (`privateEndorsement: true`), at the cost of a slower, MACI-gated petition.
- **Public representative votes.** An *elected representative* voting on behalf of members votes
  **publicly and attributably**. Secret ballots are for citizens, never for officials. Accountability
  runs in exactly one direction, and inverting it is how transparency becomes surveillance.

## Consequences

**Good**
- Vote-buying is not policed, it is made *uneconomic*, which is the only enforcement that scales.
- A coerced voter has a real, silent, usable defence.
- Tallies stay publicly verifiable — transparency of *outcome* with privacy of *ballot*.

**Bad / accepted risk**
- **Complexity.** MACI is genuinely hard: circuits, a message queue, a batched tally, and a
  committee ceremony per election. It is the single largest engineering cost in this design, and it
  is why the roadmap ships public-tally governance first behind a flag and switches MACI on in
  Phase 3 rather than pretending it is a v1 line item.
- **Tally latency.** Batched proving means results arrive in minutes-to-hours, not instantly.
  Acceptable — and honestly, a democratic system that resists the pressure for instant results is
  better for it.
- **Committee liveness is a hard dependency.** If ≥3 of 7 vanish, the tally stalls. Mitigations:
  over-provision (m=7, t=5), slashable liveness attestations, per-election resampling, and a
  **documented last-resort fallback** in which the election is re-run under a fresh committee with
  the encrypted message queue intact — never a fallback to a plaintext tally, which would retroactively
  strip privacy from voters who already voted.
- **A briber can still buy the *account*** (buy the phone, the passkey, the guardian set). This is
  the residual attack. Mitigations: non-exportable enclave keys (ADR-002), personhood re-attestation
  on device change, and anomaly detection on bulk device-transfer patterns. It is not fully solvable
  and we say so in Doc 01 rather than claiming a clean win.

## Alternatives considered

- **Plain ZK anonymous voting (Semaphore signal only).** Anonymous but *not* receipt-free — the
  voter can reveal their secret to prove their vote. Rejected as the vote mechanism; retained for
  non-coercible signals.
- **JCJ / Civitas fake-credential schemes.** Theoretically the strongest coercion resistance (a
  coerced voter hands over a *fake* credential). Rejected for v1: quadratic tally cost, and a UX
  ("remember which of your credentials is the real one") that is unusable by the target population —
  a coercion defence nobody can operate is not a defence.
- **Homomorphic tallying with threshold decryption, no key-change.** Gives ballot privacy and a
  verifiable tally, but **not** receipt-freeness against a voter who reveals their randomness.
  Rejected — it solves the observer and leaves the briber.
